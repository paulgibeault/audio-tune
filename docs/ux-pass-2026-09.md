# The UX pass — design

*2026-09-12, after the first real use. Direction: "the sound boards could use
customization — ordering, assigning, what is visible, under a menu. Lessen
friction all around. The controls and charts look great; their function is
confusing. Group and organize them so a newcomer is gently introduced and
everything stays immediately available to the experienced user. Explore and
Compose are daunting. And fleet packs must be read-only."*

## What was wrong, in one list

- **Play** had no way to reorder, hide or resize pads, and a fleet board's
  tools (stop beds, save a copy, share, send, export, delete) sat in a flat
  row of buttons above the grid.
- **Explore › Fleet sounds** started with two dropdowns. A cue then unrolled
  into every layer with every parameter at once — a ten-layer cue is forty
  controls — plus a timeline, a transport with seven things in it, the
  game's per-play parameters labelled "the game passes:", and the pack code.
  Nothing said which of those forty controls matter.
- **Compose** opened on the song's file management: two rows of eleven
  buttons (new, duplicate, delete, undo, redo, share, send, export, import,
  render, render ×4) before the transport or the grid. Adding a track meant
  two dropdowns and a button at the very bottom. A one-pattern song still
  showed a chain to edit.
- **Read-only.** Tweaking a fleet recipe never wrote to the pack (verified:
  an untouched offline render hashes identically before and after tweaking
  every layer through the view), but nothing in the UI *said* so, tweaks
  had nowhere to go, and reopening a sound recorded a new take, so values
  that "vary per play" looked different from the ones just left behind. It
  read as the pack having changed.

## The principles

1. **One thing first.** Every view opens on the thing you do most — pads,
   a sound, the grid — and the management lives behind one `⋯` menu.
2. **Essentials, then everything.** A layer shows the controls that make it
   *this* sound (pitch, length, level, and anything the pack varies per
   play). The rest sits under *More* on the card. One switch in Explore,
   **Show all controls**, turns every card fully open and stays on: the
   experienced user sets it once.
3. **Controls are grouped by what they do to the sound**, in the same order
   on every card: **Pitch** · **Shape** · **Tone** · **Level**. A group
   caption is one word; after a few cards the order is muscle memory.
4. **Pick sounds one way.** Game chips, then sound chips, tap to hear. The
   same picker chooses a sound in Explore, assigns a pad, and adds a track.
5. **Say what is read-only, and give tweaks a home.** A fleet recipe carries
   a line: *the game's pack; can't be changed here — your changes live in
   this session*. **Save as my sound** turns the take, with your changes,
   into a cue in My sounds: a pad, a track, and pack code, all yours.
6. **Introduce once.** Each view has a short first-time card (three lines,
   Got it) instead of the single welcome wall, keyed per view so it only
   appears where you are.

## Play — customizing a board

Every board, fleet or mine, has a **Customize** toggle and a `⋯` menu.

- **Customize on:** pads grow handles. Drag a pad onto another to move it;
  ◀ ▶ on each pad do the same from the keyboard. The eye hides a pad; hidden
  pads stay in the grid, dimmed, while customizing, and are gone when it
  is off. On my boards, tapping a pad's body assigns its sound (the pad
  editor); on fleet boards the sounds are the pack's, so tapping opens the
  settings sheet as before.
- **The menu:** pad size (small / regular / large), key hints on or off,
  reset layout (fleet), stop beds, save a copy, and — on my boards —
  share, send, export, delete.
- **Where it lives.** A fleet board's layout is `{ order, hidden }` by cue
  name in its own store (`layouts`, keyed by pack id); the pack itself is
  never touched, and a cue the pack adds later appears at the end. A user
  board's `pads[]` is the order; a hidden pad carries `hidden: true` and
  rides share codes and files.
- **Keys follow the visible order:** the number row is always the first ten
  pads you can see.

## Explore — the recipe, introduced

- **Picking:** game chips in the fleet's colours, then the game's sounds as
  chips; tap to hear and open. My sounds is not in this list — it has its
  own tab.
- **The recipe head:** name, the pack's note, one big Play, Original,
  Re-roll, Reset, the take number, and the read-only line with **Save as my
  sound**. WAV and Copy code move under `⋯`. "The game passes" becomes
  **Game parameters** with a one-line explanation.
- **Layer cards:** a summary line (*strike · 20 ms click above 1.4 kHz ·
  −13 dB*), then the essentials in Pitch · Shape · Tone · Level order, then
  *More (n)*. The timeline stays; the pack code is a collapsed section at
  the end.
- **Elements and My sounds** use the same grouping and the same switch.

## Compose — grid first

- The head is the song's name, saved state, Undo/Redo and a `⋯` menu (songs
  list, new, duplicate, delete, share, send, export, import, render).
- The transport is Play / Record / metronome / position / tempo + Tap.
  Swing, steps per bar and bars sit under **Grid ▾**.
- **Add a sound** is one primary button on the grid card; it opens the
  shared picker as a sheet. An empty song shows that button and nothing
  else to learn.
- The chain appears once there are two patterns. With one, a hint says what
  a second pattern would give you.

## What stays exactly as it was

The controls themselves, their pictures and help; the recorder and the
override path; the scheduler; the stores' shapes (a board file from before
this pass loads unchanged); every keyboard shortcut.
